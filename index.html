My Process

How I approach making things

01

Observe

I've started paying a lot more attention to how light falls on things, how colors interact, and what makes a composition feel balanced. I spend time looking before I start.

02

Experiment

A lot of what I make comes from just trying something and seeing what happens. I'm still learning, so accidents often teach me more than plans do.

03

Refine

Once I have something I like, I try to figure out why it works — or doesn't. That reflection is where I've grown the most as someone who never thought of themselves as artistic.

Artist Statement

I didn't expect to like art. When I found out I'd be taking an art class, I was honestly pretty skeptical. I've never considered myself creative in that way — I'm more drawn to chemistry, coding, and how systems work.

But somewhere in learning about color theory, I started to understand that art has its own logic. Complementary colors, value, composition — there are rules, and then there are the interesting things that happen when you break them. That's when it clicked for me.

I'm still very much a beginner, and I think that's okay. Most of what I make is an experiment. I'm less interested in making something "good" than in understanding why something works — which, honestly, is how I approach most things I'm into.

Brayden — CASC, Grade 11 — Class of 2027
